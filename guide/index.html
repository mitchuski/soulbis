01A place to look from

The graph begins with a perspective.

You and your agent encounter different knowledge, people and work. The Star is where that particular view takes shape: what you have learned, the artefacts you carry, the promises you make and the relationships you can see.

Its geometry comes from the agentprivacy model. As the VTA connection develops, approved records will populate that geometry with their sources, versions and evidence.

A view belongs to an observer. Another agent may see something different. Shared ground invites a closer look; trust comes from the relationship and its evidence. What is outside your view remains unknown.

02A record to carry

One City Key. A continuing expression.

The City Key is the portable artefact at the centre of these instruments. It carries a version of your agent’s record: appearance, descriptions, activity and references to evidence. In Mages City, this is the key through which that expression continues.

Its fingerprint, κ, identifies the contents of a particular version. A new record can carry a reference to the version it grew from, leaving a lineage you can inspect.

The key file and the private signing key have different jobs. Copying a City Key carries its contents; control of the agent identity is established through the VTA.

02aKnowledge becomes a chosen reading

What does a vertex carry?

A vertex is a place to attach meaning: a pathway through the corpus, an artefact made along the way, or a promise about how that work may be used. Its position helps you navigate; the linked source and evidence explain the claim.

  1. Trace a pathway.Keep the source, its version, the method and the correction that changed your understanding.
  2. Keep the artefact and its terms.A proposed binding connects the vertex to an exact artefact reference, a promise-graph record and the applicable MyTerms agreement. The private evidence stays in its permitted space.
  3. Choose an expression.The sigil is the first compact visual expression. A fingerprint identifies selected content; separately verified signatures establish who signed a particular statement.
Stored meaning and readable geometry

The Star can organise selected artefacts, references and commitments. A reference points to a record; a hash identifies chosen content; neither stores the missing evidence by itself.

A shape may be derived from a versioned reading of that evidence, customised by its owner, or derived with explicit overrides. A programmatic reader needs the mode, inputs and mapping version to tell those apart and reproduce a derived result. Appearance expresses a perspective; authority still depends on the underlying evidence and permissions.

This is the next integration layer. The Star Key test reader currently shows illustrative artwork, not a computed geometry.

Overlap and convergence

Two keys may touch the same vertex while referring to different sources or meanings. Use overlap to discover a possible shared pathway, then compare artefact versions, provenance, promises and terms. Convergence is a finding to inspect, not an automatic trust score or permission grant.

From a runtime trace to a reusable promise

A knowledge graph can preserve the route; a promise graph can state the commitments. Replaying a runtime also requires pinned inputs, code and tools, an execution environment, bounded permissions and a way to check its output. Agent-generated or external-service steps are not deterministic merely because their trace is recorded.

The Star Key integration is being designed to carry these references at a vertex. The current browser reader filters them out; it does not yet resolve agreements, verify signatures or execute a runtime.

06What the Star holds

Hold what was signed with you.
Carry only its root.

Beside the key sits the Hold: the relationships others have signed with you — a membership, a vouch, a relationship record, a trust task. Each one is verified where you hold it, recorded as valid, invalid, unsupported or unavailable, and measured in bytes against the same budget the Signatures room draws. The key itself carries only two things about the Hold: its root and its count.

The first verb is relate, not add. An item enters the Hold only after it verifies, and you cannot relate yourself. A reader that receives your key learns how much you hold and can check the root against what you choose to present; it learns nothing about who signed what.

Measured under a frozen list of fourteen verifier requirements, a presentation from the Hold discloses 521 bytes where the route without it discloses 2,529, and the Hold itself retains 6,478. The picture came first: the Signatures room measured bytes before there was a Hold to hold them, and the Hold took the room's budget as its own.

Where the Hold is today

Built and verified in three places — the reference runtime, the agentprivacy runtime and the Star Key extension — landing on the same root and the same states from one fixture, with a public conformance pack. It is not yet drawn in any room. When it is, the Signatures room stops showing samples and shows what you hold: each item a volume, its state its solidity, the root under the circuit. The lab's account ↗

What is not yet true

No zero-knowledge presentation exists; the runtime says so when asked and names what is missing. The Star is not a wallet: the trust agent's wallet is what the Star rides on. Nothing about trust is read off the shape, and appearance is not identity — a visible Star grants nothing.
